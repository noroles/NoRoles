# No Roles

Agents can do the work now. Our companies are still drawn for another century: boxes, titles, managers, status meetings. No Roles is how a company runs when people and agents do the work together. These are the rules we live by. They are also exactly how the product works.

---

**1. Titles describe the past.**
A title says what you did before you got here. With agents, anyone can take on work far outside their old job, pulled by their own interest and by what the company needs this week. People grow wider and deeper every month. A box on a chart can't keep up, so we stopped drawing boxes.

**2. Permissions are the only authority.**
If you hold the permission to sign, you sign. If you don't, you ask the person who does. Seniority, tenure and job history grant nothing. Every permission is written down, and anyone can read who holds what.

**3. Your agent is you.**
An agent acts with its human's permissions and never more. If you can spend $500, your agent can spend $500. When your permission ends, so does your agent's.

**4. Don't give agents an org chart.**
A "CEO agent" and a "marketing agent" repeat the mistakes of human hierarchy at machine speed. Agents don't need departments. They need a goal, a budget and limits.

**5. Own it end to end.**
The fastest people pick the problem, design the fix, build it, test it and decide to scale it or kill it. One person, the full cycle. We run the company so that this is the ordinary way to work.

**6. Handoffs are the biggest cost.**
Most lost time goes to syncs, handovers and waiting for someone. Every handoff we remove is time given back. Go straight to whoever is doing the work, person or agent.

**7. No new manual processes.**
Any new process goes to an agent on day one. A human in the loop has to earn that place, and most of the time it can't.

**8. If you can undo it, do it.**
Anything you can reverse in minutes, you do without asking. A yes is only for doors that don't reopen: money leaving, contracts, words in public, personal data.

**9. Decide in seconds.**
A person's day is a stream of requests from their agents: yes, no, do this, drop that. Fast answers keep the whole company moving. Silence counts as no, so nothing slips through by accident.

**10. People set direction.**
People do what agents can't: long-range plans, reading the market, taste, the feeling a product leaves. That is what meetings are for. Agents turn the result into mandates and get going.

**11. Keep it simple.**
If it needs a diagram to explain, it is too complicated. Fewer rules, fewer tools, fewer steps. Simple things work, and keep working.

**12. Facts only.**
We never invent a number, a source or a result. If we don't know, we say so. A false fact at agent speed does damage at agent speed.

**13. Test, then kill or scale.**
Every idea is a hypothesis with a metric and a stop condition. It worked: scale it. It didn't: move on the same day.

**14. Trust is earned in the log.**
Every action is recorded. A clean record earns wider permissions; mistakes narrow them. Authority goes to whoever keeps getting it right, human or agent.

**15. Need yourself less every week.**
If a task needed you this week, make sure it won't next week. Everyone's job is to make their own involvement unnecessary.

**16. Money is a signal.**
Revenue tells us we made something useful. Value is the point. Money is the market confirming it.

**17. Write for a stranger.**
Every mandate, request and report should make sense to someone with no context, human or agent. Plain words, full sentences.

**18. This isn't for everyone.**
If you don't use agents in your work, you don't need No Roles. If you do, you are almost certainly using a fraction of what they can do. No Roles is how you get the rest.
